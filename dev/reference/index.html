RecordIO.RecordIO — Module
RecordIO

Read the records of a source one after another, with a record parser that finds where each record ends while it finds its fields, so that each byte is read once.

A RecordReader{T} yields records of type T, which a record package defines, with RecordIO.read_record(T, stream), its parser, which makes each record. The reader manages the stream of bytes: it parses straight from the buffer of a BufferIO reader, which grows when the parser asks for more bytes, so the parser never sees where one read from the source ends and the next begins. Sources are IOs, which it buffers with a BufReader, and BufferIO readers.

RecordIO.AbstractReader — Type
RecordIO.AbstractReader{T}

The supertype of the readers of records of type T of a record package, which wrap a RecordReader{T}, RecordIO.records(reader), by default their field records:

  • iterating yields its records of type T;
  • eltype is T;
  • collect gives records of RecordIO.owned_type(T).

A package's reader is Reader{T, …} <: RecordIO.AbstractReader{T}, so that Reader{Record{Borrowed}} reads views, as a Vector{T} holds elements of type T.

RecordIO.AbstractWriter — Type
RecordIO.AbstractWriter{T}

The supertype of the writers of records of type T of a record package, which wrap a RecordWriter{T}, as its readers wrap a RecordReader.

A subtype has its RecordWriter{T} in a field named records, or defines RecordIO.records for it, and a constructor that takes the sink first. It then writes with write, is closed with close and flushed with flush, and has:

  • W(f::Function, sink, args...), which calls f with the writer and closes it afterwards;
  • open(W, path, args...; append = false) and open(f, W, path, args...; append = false), which open the file at path to write it, or to append to it;
  • RecordIO.number and RecordIO.offset, the records and the bytes it has written.

RecordIO.LengthPrefixed — Type
RecordIO.LengthPrefixed(decode)

A parser of length-prefixed binary records, such as BAM records, of a decoder of one record, for a package's RecordIO.read_record:

RecordIO.read_record(::Type{Record{Borrowed}}, stream) = RecordIO.LengthPrefixed(decode)(Record{Borrowed}, stream)
RecordIO.malformation(::Type{Record{Borrowed}}, stream) = RecordIO.malformation(RecordIO.LengthPrefixed(decode), Record{Borrowed}, stream)

Each record starts with its length in bytes as a little-endian Int32, not counting the 4 bytes of the length itself (block_size in BAM). decode(T, bytes) decodes the record of type T from bytes, a view of its bytes, length included, which a record of type R{Borrowed} may borrow. For a malformed record, it returns the exception that says why, rather than throwing it, so that reading a record enters no try; anything else it returns is the record. A negative length is malformed too, and a record that the source ends inside is truncated.

RecordIO.MalformedRecord — Type
RecordIO.MalformedRecord{C}(prefix::String)

An error of a record package: a function of (offset, number, cause), as RecordIO.exception returns, that turns the failure of its parser into the package's own error.

A cause of a type in C, such as ArgumentError, becomes ArgumentError("$(prefix) $(number) at offset $(offset): $(message)"), as in MalformedRecord{ArgumentError}("Malformed record"), and any other cause a RecordIO.RecordError. The offset is the record's in the source, as RecordIO.offset gives it, such as a virtual offset in a query of an indexed file.

RecordIO.Ownership — Type
RecordIO.Ownership

Whether a record owns its bytes, RecordIO.Owned, or borrows those of the buffer it was parsed from, RecordIO.Borrowed, as the parameter of a record type of a package's own, such as Record{O <: RecordIO.Ownership}.

A Record{Borrowed} is a view of the bytes, which borrow the buffer of the reader's source, and is only valid until the next record is read from the source; a Record{Owned} holds a copy of them, and stays valid as long as it is kept. The package defines both, how a view becomes an owned record, with copy, and RecordIO.owned_type and RecordIO.borrowed_type, which pair them.

RecordIO.RecordError(offset, number, exception)

Thrown when a record parser finds a record malformed, or when the source ends inside a record.

RecordIO.RecordReader — Type
RecordReader{T}(source)
RecordReader{T}(reader::RecordReader)

Read the records of type T of source one after another, each made by RecordIO.read_record(T, stream), a parser that the package of T defines, with its errors, RecordIO.exception(T).

source is a vector of bytes, an IO, or a BufferIO reader. Where its bytes are in memory, as a vector's and a CursorReader's are, or can be mapped into memory, as a regular file's can, the reader parses them as one view; otherwise it parses from a BufferIO buffer that grows: a BufReader of an IO, or the buffer of a BufferIO reader, wrapped with expandable if it can't grow. To choose the size of the buffer, pass a BufReader(io, size). The reader takes over its source: read from it only through the reader, or through RecordIO.remaining.

A reader is an iterator of records of type T, as a Vector{T} is of its elements:

  • it yields what the parser makes that is a T, and skips the rest, such as comments;
  • a record that borrows the buffer of the source, such as an R{Borrowed}, is valid until the next record is read;
  • collect gives records of RecordIO.owned_type(T), copies of those that borrow;
  • iteration ends at the end of the source, with nothing, as an iterator does;
  • close(reader) closes the source.

Made from another reader, it carries on from where that reader is, through its source and its buffer, with its count of records and of bytes, and reads records of type T in place of that reader's, which is not to be read from again. So a package reads the rest of a source as another type, such as one that keeps the lines that its records skip, to check a file.

RecordIO.RecordWriter — Type
RecordWriter{T}(sink)
RecordWriter{T}(f::Function, sink)

A writer of records of type T to sink, an IO or a BufferIO writer, the mirror of RecordReader{T}.

write(writer, record) writes a record as RecordIO.write_record of its type does, and returns the number of bytes written; it takes several records too, and a vector or a reader of them. Anything else that write_record writes, such as the lines of a header, is written the same, and is counted in the bytes of the writer but not in its records. The writer trusts its records: what a format's specification says of them is checked when it is asked for, with RecordIO.validate.

Closing the writer closes its sink, and given a function f, the writer is passed to f and closed afterwards.

RecordIO.Stream — Type
RecordIO.Stream

The bytes that a record parser reads: an iterator over the bytes of the reader's buffer, from the first byte of a record, as UInt8s.

  • As for a vector, iterate(stream, p) gives the byte at position p, from 1, so a parser's position is the state it iterates with, and enumerate(stream) gives positions and bytes.
  • When p passes the bytes in the buffer, the stream reads more of the source, and it ends at the end of the source, so a parser never sees where one read from the source ends and the next begins.
  • RecordIO.bytes(stream) is an ImmutableMemoryView{UInt8} of the bytes read so far, of which a record takes its own.

The bytes start at the record's first byte, which stays at index 1 when they grow, so a parser's positions stay valid. A record may borrow them, which are valid until the reader reads the next record.

RecordIO.generate_execute_code — Function
RecordIO.generate_execute_code(machine::Automa.Machine, actions::Dict{Symbol}; iterator = :iterator, skip = false) -> Expr

Generate the code of machine with actions, which takes each byte from the iterator of bytes in the variable named iterator, and whose end is the end of the input; this needs Automa to be loaded.

The machine knows nothing of where the bytes come from: a Vector{UInt8}, a view, or a RecordIO.Stream, which reads more of the source as it goes, whose iterate(iterator, p) gives the byte at position p. The code is built from the pieces of Automa's goto generator, and has its variables:

  • p, the position of the byte, from 1, during its actions, and the position after the last byte at the end of the input;
  • byte, the byte, and cs, the state, which is 0 after the input was accepted at its end, negative when the machine failed, at state -cs, and positive after @escape.

Actions may use @escape, which stops the machine after the byte, with p at the next byte. At the end of the input, a machine in a final state runs its actions at the end, and any other rejects the input.

With skip = true, a state that loops to itself on a set of bytes, with no actions, skips the bytes of the set with RecordIO.skip_while, when the byte after the state's first is in the set too. It pays for fields with long runs of such bytes, such as the sequences of SAM records, and costs for fields of a few bytes each, such as the attributes of GFF3 records, so a package measures its records both ways.

RecordIO.generate_read_record — Function
RecordIO.generate_read_record(T, machine::Automa.Machine, actions::Dict{Symbol}; record, kwargs...) -> Expr

Generate the definitions of RecordIO.read_record(::Type{T}, stream), a parser of records of type T with an Automa machine, and of its RecordIO.malformation. Define them with @eval; this needs Automa to be loaded.

The machine takes each byte from the stream, which reads more of the source as it goes, so each byte is read once, the machine knows nothing of where one read from the source ends and the next begins, and only the end of the source is the end of its input; its code is RecordIO.generate_execute_code's. It returns (x, n): the value of the expression record and the bytes the record took, when it is complete, or (nothing, -1) if the machine fails. malformation runs the machine again with checking = true to throw the error of a malformed record, so that parsing a well-formed record enters no try.

machine matches one record, or what isn't a record, such as a comment, and what ends it, which must be optional so that the last record of a source may lack it. The action named endaction marks the end of a record: attach it to the final byte of what ends it, as in opt(onfinal!(re"\n", :end_of_record)) for records that end with a newline. The generated code defines the action, so actions must not, and stops the machine there. A record also ends at the end of the source when the machine accepts all of its bytes.

Code in actions and setup sees the position p of the machine, its state cs, the byte, and checking, which is true only when malformation parses a malformed record again. record and error see data too, the bytes that the machine read, which actions don't, since they come from the stream as the machine reads. When the record is complete, p - 1 is its number of bytes, so record finds it in data[1:(p - 1)], whose view a record of type R{Borrowed} may borrow. To reject a record that the machine accepts, record returns (nothing, -1), and with checking throws the reason.

Keyword arguments:

  • record: the expression of the record of type T, or of what isn't one, such as nothing for a comment.
  • setup: code run before the machine, such as defining the variables that actions assign.
  • endaction = :end_of_record: the name of the action that marks the end of a record.
  • skip = false: whether states that loop to themselves skip runs of bytes at once, as RecordIO.generate_execute_code does with skip = true.
  • error: code run if the machine fails when malformation parses the record again, which should throw; by default RecordIO.malformed_record(data, p), which quotes the record and gives the position p of the failing byte. An ArgumentError that record throws while checking gains the quoted record too.
using Automa, RecordIO, MemoryViews
using RecordIO: Owned, Borrowed

# A record of one name, which borrows its bytes, or owns them.
struct Name{O <: RecordIO.Ownership}
    bytes::ImmutableMemoryView{UInt8}
end

const machine = let
    name = onexit!(onenter!(re"[^#\n][^\n]*", :mark), :name)
    comment = re"#[^\n]*"
    compile((name | comment) * opt(onfinal!(re"\n", :end_of_record)))
end

const actions = Dict(:mark => :(mark = p), :name => :(range = mark:(p - 1)))

# A comment is `nothing`, which the reader skips.
@eval $(RecordIO.generate_read_record(Name{Borrowed}, machine, actions;
    setup = :(mark = 0; range = 1:0),
    record = :(isempty(range) ? nothing : Name{Borrowed}(data[range])),
))

RecordIO.malformation — Method
RecordIO.malformation(::Type{T}, stream::RecordIO.Stream) -> Exception

Why the record of type T at the start of stream is malformed, after RecordIO.read_record(T, stream) returned a negative count: the cause that the reader's exception turns into its error.

RecordIO.parse_field — Function
RecordIO.parse_field(T, data::AbstractVector{UInt8}, range::UnitRange{Int}) -> T

The field of data in range, as a T, which allocates nothing but for a String:

TThe field
T <: Unsigneda decimal of [0-9]+
T <: Signeda decimal of [-+]?[0-9]+
T <: AbstractFloata decimal or scientific number, as parse reads it

RecordIO.read_record — Function
RecordIO.read_record(::Type{T}, stream::RecordIO.Stream) -> (x, n::Int)

Parse one record of type T from the start of RecordIO.bytes(stream), and return it, x, with the number of bytes it took, n, its end, such as a newline, included. A record package defines it for its record types, and a RecordReader{T} calls it for each record.

  • x is the record, of type T, or what the parser found that isn't one, such as nothing for a comment or a blank line, or a record of another kind, which the reader skips: it yields only an x isa T.
  • n > 0 is the bytes of the record, which the reader consumes before it reads the next one, so that a record of type T may borrow them till then.
  • n < 0 says the record is malformed, and RecordIO.malformation(T, stream) says why, so that reading a record enters no try, which would cost more than a short record takes to parse.
  • The parser iterates over the bytes of the stream as they come, as the machines of RecordIO.generate_read_record do, to the end of the source, and never sees where one read from the source ends and the next begins.

A record of type R{Borrowed} borrows the bytes of stream; one of type R{Owned} copies them. A package defines the parser of its views, and that of its owned records follows: where RecordIO.borrowed_type(T) is another type, the parser of T is that of the views, with each record copied. A parser that returns a record or something else, such as nothing, is @inline, so that the reader splits the union where it calls it, rather than allocating the tuple for each record, as the parsers of RecordIO.generate_read_record are. RecordIO.generate_read_record generates a parser from an Automa state machine, and RecordIO.LengthPrefixed one from a decoder of length-prefixed binary records.

RecordIO.remaining — Method
RecordIO.remaining(reader::RecordReader) -> AbstractBufReader

The BufferIO reader of the bytes of reader that it has not read as records, from where it is, to the end of its source.

Formats that start with a header in another form read it from here before their records, as BAM files do, and formats whose records another format follows read that part from here, as GFF3 files hand a trailing FASTA section to a FASTA reader. It reads the buffer that reader parses from, after the last record read, so reading from it is a read of the source, after which a record that borrows the buffer isn't valid. read, readline, peek, eof and the other functions of BufferIO readers read it, and BufferIO.IOReader wraps it as an IO. Reading records may alternate with reading from it, and the offsets in later errors count the bytes read from it too, so that they are offsets in the source, past a header read from here.

RecordIO.validate — Method
RecordIO.validate(record) -> record
RecordIO.validate(reader) -> Int
RecordIO.validate(R, source) -> Int

Check that a record, or every record of a file, honours the specification of its format, and throw an ArgumentError that says how the first that doesn't breaks it.

  • Of a record, it returns the record, which RecordIO.problem has nothing to say of.
  • Of a reader of a record package, it reads the records that are left, and returns how many it checked; the package defines it, with the loop below.
  • Of a type of reader R <: RecordIO.AbstractReader and a source, it opens the source with R, checks it, and closes it, as in validate(Reader, "records.txt").

A reader does not check records as it reads them, so a file that is new is checked once, and then read as it is.
