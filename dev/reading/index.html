Reading records

A RecordReader{T} reads records of type T from a source, with the parser of the record type, and is an iterator of them, as a Vector{T} is. The examples read lines with the Line record type of Record types, whose parser gives nothing for a comment, which the reader skips.

text_of_lines = join(["# a header", "first", "second", "# between", "third"], '\n') * '\n'
reader = RecordReader{Line{Owned}}(IOBuffer(text_of_lines))
eltype(reader), [String(text(line)) for line in reader]
(Main.Line{RecordIO.Owned}, ["first", "second", "third"])

Iteration ends at the end of the source, and close(reader) closes the source.

Owned records and views

The record type says what iteration yields:

  • R{Owned}: records of their own, each with a copy of its bytes, which stay as they are after the next record is read;
  • R{Borrowed}: views of the reader's buffer, which allocate nothing for each, and are valid until the next record is read.

The owned records are copies of the views, so iterating over a reader of R{Owned} is Iterators.map(copy, views). Reading a field as a view, such as text, allocates nothing either, so what a reader of views allocates is the reader itself, with the buffer of its source, whatever the number of records:

total_length(reader) = sum(line -> ncodeunits(text(line)), reader)
views(lines) = RecordReader{LineView}(IOBuffer(lines))
total_length(views(text_of_lines)) # compile it
few, many = text_of_lines, text_of_lines^100
(@allocated total_length(views(few))) == (@allocated total_length(views(many)))
false

Keeping records

collect copies every view, so that a collected vector holds owned records, of RecordIO.owned_type of the reader's type:

collected = collect(views(text_of_lines))
eltype(collected), String.(text.(collected))
(Main.Line{RecordIO.Owned}, ["first", "second", "third"])

A comprehension, map or push! of views keeps views of bytes that later reads move or overwrite, so copy each as it comes to keep it:

copies = [copy(line) for line in views(text_of_lines)]
String.(text.(copies))
3-element Vector{String}:
 "first"
 "second"
 "third"

Who owns which bytes

Each buffer has one owner, and is valid while its owner leaves it as it is:

BufferOwnerValid
the bytes of the parser's streamthe buffer of the reader's sourceuntil the next record is read: consuming a record and refilling the buffer move its bytes, and growing it replaces its memory
a viewthe buffer of the reader's sourceuntil the next record is read
an owned record's bytesthe recordas long as the record; copy(view) copies the bytes of the record, and no more
the sourcethe readerfrom when the reader is made until it is closed; read it only through the reader, or RecordIO.remaining

The bytes are Julia memory, and a view keeps its memory alive, so a view kept too long doesn't read freed memory: it reads the bytes that its owner has put there since.

Reading one record at a time

RecordIO.read_next(reader) reads the next record, skipping what isn't one, and returns nothing at the end of the source. Iteration reads through it, and it is how a package reads records itself, as a query of an indexed file does, which stops at the first record past its region.

reader = RecordReader{LineView}(IOBuffer("first\n# a trailing comment\n"))
first_line = RecordIO.read_next(reader)
String(text(first_line)), RecordIO.read_next(reader)
("first", nothing)

The readers of a package

A package's reader is parameterised on the records it yields, Reader{T} <: RecordIO.AbstractReader{T}, as a Vector{T} is on its elements, with a field records, the RecordReader{T} of its source, which gives it iteration, eltype and collect: RecordIO.AbstractReader. So Reader{Record{Owned}} yields owned records and Reader{Record{Borrowed}} views, and collect gives owned records either way.

struct LineReader{T, R <: RecordReader{T}} <: RecordIO.AbstractReader{T}
    records::R
end

LineReader{T}(source) where {T} = (records = RecordReader{T}(source); LineReader{T, typeof(records)}(records))

reader = LineReader{LineView}(IOBuffer(text_of_lines))
eltype(reader), eltype(collect(reader))
(Main.Line{RecordIO.Borrowed}, Main.Line{RecordIO.Owned})

A reader that ends its records before the end of its source, as one of a format whose records another format follows does, defines iterate itself, with RecordIO.read_next of its records, and collect then gives what it yields.

The bytes that aren't records

RecordIO.remaining(reader) is the BufferIO reader of the bytes that the reader hasn't read as records, from where it is: the buffer that the reader parses from. A format that starts with a header in another form reads it from there before its records, as BAM files do:

reader = RecordReader{Line{Owned}}(IOBuffer("HEADER:4\nfirst\nsecond\n"))
header = readline(RecordIO.remaining(reader))
header, [String(text(line)) for line in reader]
("HEADER:4", ["first", "second"])

Reading from it moves the reader on, so the records continue after the bytes read, and reading from it may alternate with reading records.

A format that ends in another one stops reading records where the other starts, and reads the rest from there, as GFF3 files hand a trailing FASTA section to a FASTA reader:

reader = RecordReader{Line{Owned}}(IOBuffer("first\nsecond\nEND\nanother format\n"))
lines = String[]
while (line = RecordIO.read_next(reader)) !== nothing && text(line) != "END"
    push!(lines, String(text(line)))
end
lines, read(RecordIO.remaining(reader), String)
(["first", "second"], "another format\n")
